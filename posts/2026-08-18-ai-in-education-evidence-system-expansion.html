AI in Education: From Sentiment to Evidence

The project expanded from a narrow sentiment question into a multi-source analysis that can compare perceptions with measurable business, occupation, employment, and wage evidence.

What changed

The question became bigger than opinion.

Student and educator attitudes remain one evidence layer. Business adoption, occupational exposure, employment, wages, and long-run educational outcomes are treated separately so agreement and contradiction remain visible.

Current evidence

23,218 student records and 174 variables, Census business adoption data, O*NET occupation and task structure, published AI-exposure measures, and BLS employment and wage data for 2021 through 2025.

A preliminary pattern

71.4% adoption

Among respondents answering the adoption question, 71.4% reported having used ChatGPT.

63.4% access benefit

Access to knowledge and study efficiency were among the stronger perceived benefits.

36.0% critical thinking

Respondents were much less convinced that ChatGPT improves critical-thinking skills.

Concern remains visible

Cheating, plagiarism, and inaccurate information still received substantial agreement.

Analytical question

Does theoretical exposure line up with actual job outcomes?

Occupation exposure measures can be compared with BLS employment and wage trends to test displacement, augmentation, slower hiring, or little detectable effect.

Research discipline

Exposure is not replacement. Correlation is not causation. Survey attitudes are not objective outcomes. Claims should remain no stronger than the evidence supporting them.
