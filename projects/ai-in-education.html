AI in Education: Evidence Before Conclusions

A research-design project focused on a problem that appears constantly in real analytics work: multiple sources seem related, but differences in population, wording, timing, definitions, and sampling can make a simple comparison invalid.

Core research rules

Do not pool incompatible surveys

Similar topics do not make different instruments statistically interchangeable.

Track definitions and dates

“ChatGPT,” “generative AI,” and “AI tools” can represent different exposures, especially across fast-changing survey years.

Separate attitudes from outcomes

Concern, usefulness, adoption, learning, and employment outcomes are different constructs and require different evidence.

Avoid causal overreach

Association, exposure, and timing do not prove that AI caused an observed outcome.

The Problem

A large dataset can still support a bad conclusion.

The project treats source compatibility and analytical validity as first-class work rather than something hidden in a footnote after the charts are finished.

Population

Who was actually surveyed?

Students, parents, teachers, and workers can answer the same-looking question from completely different contexts. Direct comparisons require compatible samples.

Instrument

Was the same thing actually measured?

Question wording, response scales, technology definitions, and survey purpose can turn apparent agreement into false equivalence.

Time

Did the environment change between surveys?

AI adoption changes quickly. Survey year and technology availability are confounders, not incidental metadata.

Comparison hierarchy

Strongest evidence first.

Strongest: same survey program, same period, parallel questions.

Very strong: paired or directly linked stakeholder samples.

Useful with warnings: separate surveys with closely matched wording and populations.

Descriptive only: different wording, countries, years, or definitions that cannot support direct statistical pooling.

Confounders tracked

AI familiarity, usage frequency, age, education level, socioeconomic status, geography, school policy, teacher training, purpose of use, survey year, and the specific technology definition can all change the interpretation.

Public Evidence

The repository now emphasizes method over raw data volume.

The public version intentionally avoids bulk user-generated text, private working journals, ambiguous third-party raw-data redistribution, and legacy scripts that do not meet the current publication standard.

Research design note

A source and comparison framework

Documents candidate evidence types, validity threats, statistical methods, and how different stakeholder sources should be compared.

Data boundary

Provenance before publication

The project documents expected inputs while keeping raw third-party data out of the public repository unless redistribution is clearly appropriate.

Exploratory code

Repository-relative analysis

The retained analysis script uses portable project paths and explicitly distinguishes descriptive/exploratory work from causal inference.

Why It Matters

The transferable skill is analytical restraint.

In production analytics, the dangerous mistake is often not a broken query. It is a technically correct query answering the wrong question or joining evidence that should not be combined. This project demonstrates source evaluation, methodological discipline, and communication of uncertainty.
